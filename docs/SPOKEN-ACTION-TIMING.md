# How It Ticks: spoken action timing and context-to-interior views

Implemented 2026-11-19 for the active daily four-component pipeline. The writer retains original problem/solution/consequence/payoff narration and now supplies exact spoken phrases for discrete transitions. Those phrases must occur once in their own accepted beat. Independent word timestamps are measured from saved speech before rendering; missing/ambiguous cues hold the run. Source WAV/MP3 hashes and accepted cue intervals are retained. Accepted per-line timing can resume without another generation request.

Renderer integration: bridge barriers/lift/passage/lowering/traffic follow measured action windows; pump cutaway, withdrawal and compression use those windows while check valves and volume remain coupled; solar cutaway and switch timing follow the spoken cue; turbine cutaway and switch follow cues while rotor/shaft/magnets keep continuous coupled rotation. Opening and ending context views are wider for panel/pump/turbine; projected whole-object bounds must fit below the hook and above the controls. Interior closeups remain connected to the same assembly. Copper coils no longer flash literally; charge carriers and magnetic fields are labeled as illustrations. Hooks are 2–7 plain words, at most 40 characters. Speech subtitles remain off in this daily renderer. Camera motion remains silent.

The daily delivery gate requires matching plan/timeline/action timing and component/overview evidence. The seal hashes those files alongside the encoded media/audio/critique, so later changes invalidate delivery. The new checkpoint namespace prevents an old plan without cues silently bypassing the new rules; cached source speech remains reusable by content hash.

Local validation: 40 pre-existing studio checks plus 11 new regression checks cover real-clock phrase binding, ambiguous/missing cues, bad timestamps, accepted-line recovery, changed audio/timelines, bridge waiting for its cue, continuous rotor coupling, valve timing, complete context framing, and rejecting clipped context views. These use engineering fixtures and make no new voice/video requests. Cloud checks and a real automatically selected/rendered video are still required.

Scope limit: the shared writing style applies to daily, cinematic and original full writers. This measured transition integration currently applies to the daily component path; cinematic/full motion binding is not being claimed complete. It is not support for arbitrary machines or a proof of natural acting, perfect physics or frame-by-frame semantic correctness. Final encoded video inspection and owner approval remain required. No Doodles run was changed or actively polled during this implementation.
